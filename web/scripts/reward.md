## You have it

> *A finite game is played for the purpose of winning. An infinite game, for
> the purpose of continuing play.*
>
> — James P. Carse, **Finite and Infinite Games**, 1986

This is not a motto bolted onto a garden. It is the rule the whole thing is
built out of, and now that you can read it, everything else in the game reads
differently:

- **You never win this.** There is no final score and no last epoch. The world
  ends only when nobody is willing to keep it, which is a failure, not a
  victory condition.
- **A finite player plays inside boundaries. An infinite player plays *with*
  them.** The well has no rule stopping you from draining it. The boundary is
  other people.
- **The requirement is hidden so that trust is the only instrument that
  works.** If you could calculate the safe draw, you would never have to talk
  to anyone. Carse's word for a game whose rules can be computed is *finite*.

## The woodland, mapped

You now know where to look. Three things hide out here, and they are hidden by
being unremarkable rather than by being far away:

| What | Where | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Seed caches** | In the undergrowth of the three clearings. They look like bushes until you are close | Seeds — lumpy, once per epoch |
| **Carved stones** | Behind the near trunks, off the walked line | One hint each |
| **This door** | The hollow you are standing in | What you are reading |

**The thing worth knowing about caches:** a thriving plot yields three seeds an
epoch, and the farm's coordination threshold is forty. Fourteen perfect
gardeners barely clear it. Three of you cannot clear it at all — not with
perfect cooperation, not ever.

So somebody has to leave the garden and come back with a cache. That is not a
side quest. **At small numbers it is the only way the commons produces
anything**, and it is why being out here costs you decay at home: the game
charges you for the trip because the trip is the point.

## And the part nobody unlocks

Whether the garden continues is not information. Nobody can tell you the safe
draw, because there isn't one — there is only what everyone else decides to
take. The phrase you just typed is the answer to the puzzle and not to the
game.
