Worldview

The camera, the tileset and one assembled scene, drawn with the actual files in art/world/. If a sprite floats or sinks here, its anchor in manifest.json is wrong — that is what this page is for.

Plate I

One scene, real assets

band: mixed

Nothing here is composited by hand. The scene is a tile map plus a list of standing objects; every sprite is placed by its manifest anchor and sorted back to front by world y. Turn on Anchors and every orange dot should sit exactly where that object meets the ground.

Plate II

The camera

The reference is the DS-era Pokémon overworld. The single most important consequence: the grid is never rotated. Rows run left to right, columns run away from you, and a tile is a rectangle.

ConstantValueWhat it is
tileW64One tile, across
tileH40The same tile in depth, after foreshortening
unitHeight40Screen pixels per world unit of height
pitchDegrees51Implied camera pitch — acos(40/64)
There is no projection matrix

tileH / tileW = 0.625 is the camera. Every ground asset is drawn already squashed, at 64×40, and the engine only places it. That is the deal that keeps the art authorable — you draw what you will see — and it is why changing either constant invalidates every ground tile in the folder rather than re-rendering them.

Things that stand up

Anything that is not ground is a billboard: upright, full height, never squashed. Squash a tree by 0.625 and you get a rug with a tree printed on it. Depth comes from two places instead — occlusion (walk behind the tree and the tree wins) and the near/far post sizes baked into prop-fence-v.svg.

Why not true perspective

A real vanishing point turns tile picking, collision and hit-testing from division into ray casts, for a difference nobody perceives across a twelve-tile view. The DS games are largely doing the same trick. If the world later needs a horizon, that is a background plate, not a change to the projection.

Plate IV

Palette

The overworld palette, from art/tokens.css. Every asset writes its colours as var(--ig-w-name, #LITERAL), so a file dropped into an <img> renders standalone and the same file inlined picks up the tokens.

Daylight is not a theme

The chrome follows the browser's light/dark setting. The world does not. Night should be something that happens in the world — an epoch, a season, a collapse — not something that happens to the browser. A garden that dims because someone changed an OS preference has told the player a lie about the game state.

The portal is deliberately ugly

Every other colour here belongs to something that grows. The portal is violet and is meant not to sit comfortably next to the beds — it is a hole in the world, and walking into one should feel slightly wrong. A later pass that makes portals blend in is a regression, not a polish.
